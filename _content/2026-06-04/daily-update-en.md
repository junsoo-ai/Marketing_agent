## SECTION 1 — GLOBAL EVENTS

**U.S. Equities Pull Back From Record Highs as Risk Appetite Softens**
The S&P 500 closed at 7,553.68 on June 3, retreating from the prior session's 7,609.78, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average settled at 50,687.07 — down sharply from 51,307.79 the day before. The Nasdaq-100 (NDX) fell 0.29% to 30,571.24, suggesting broad profit-taking after several sessions of AI-driven record highs. **Oil Prices Surge on Iran-U.S. Ceasefire Breakdown**
Brent crude closed at $98.31/bbl on June 3 after touching an intraday high of $98.68, as Iran's Tasnim news agency reported the shutdown of U.S.-Iran ceasefire negotiations. WTI continuous contract was last quoted near $96.20 (+2.60%), and the July WTI contract (CLN26) surged over 7% in a single session earlier this week. Brent reached a 52-week intraday high of $120.88 on April 30, and analysts warn the current geopolitical trajectory could retest those levels. *(MarketWatch, Barchart, Forbes Advisor, June 3, 2026)*

**Inflation Remains Stubbornly Above Fed Target; Core PCE at 3.3%**
April CPI printed at 3.8% year-over-year — the highest since May 2023 — while core CPI rose 2.8% annually, well above the Federal Reserve's 2% target. Energy prices surged 17.9% annually, accounting for more than 40% of the headline increase. The Fed's preferred gauge, core PCE, came in at 3.3% annually, keeping rate-cut expectations muted heading into summer. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh was sworn in on May 22 and has yet to signal a clear policy pivot. *(BLS CPI Release, CNBC, May 2026)*

**Australia Posts Trade Deficit as Exports Contract**
Australia's April trade balance swung to a deficit of A$-1.841 billion against a consensus expectation of a A$1.8 billion surplus, with exports falling 2.7% month-over-month. The data, released at 01:30 AM on June 4, adds to concerns about softening global demand and is being closely watched ahead of RBA commentary. Singapore's S&P Global PMI for May came in at 57.9, showing continued manufacturing expansion in Southeast Asia. *(Trading Economics Economic Calendar, June 4, 2026)*

**Gold Retreats Sharply as Dollar and Oil Command Safe-Haven Flows**
Gold futures fell $57.20 or 1.27% to $4,462.70 on the continuous contract, as rising oil prices and a firmer U.S. Dollar Index captured risk-off flows that might otherwise support bullion. Earlier analysis noted that gold had already declined roughly 13% from its highs as Brent broke above $100, pushing investors toward cash-generating equities. The 10-year Treasury yield environment remains elevated, further compressing the appeal of non-yielding assets. *(MarketWatch Futures Overview, Crux Investor, June 3, 2026)*
