## SECTION 1 — GLOBAL EVENTS

**US equities suffer their worst single-day drop in weeks as Middle East tensions and energy fears collide**
All three major US indices fell sharply on June 10, with the Dow shedding 952 points (−1.87%) to close at 49,920, the S&P 500 losing 119 points (−1.61%) to 7,268, and the Nasdaq dropping 505 points (−1.97%) to 25,169. The selloff was broad-based, with AI-adjacent hardware and energy names leading declines — Super Micro Computer cratered 25.1%, GE Vernova fell 6.8%, and Qualcomm slid 7.2%. The move reverses the tentative stabilisation seen in Wednesday's session and puts the S&P 500 down roughly 4.4% from its early-June highs. *(Economic Times / Yahoo Finance / MarketWatch, June 10–11, 2026)*

**Cleveland Fed nowcast puts June CPI at 4.05% — rate-cut window continues to narrow**
The Cleveland Fed's inflation nowcasting model, updated June 10, projects June 2026 headline CPI at 4.05% year-over-year and core CPI at 2.85%. Q2 annualised CPI is tracking at a striking 6.77%, well above the Fed's 2% target, while the Philadelphia Fed's Q2 Survey of Professional Forecasters raised its current-quarter headline CPI estimate to 6.0% annualised, up sharply from a prior 2.7% projection. Markets are pricing in no rate cuts before year-end, with the FOMC having flagged at its April 28–29 meeting that shorter-term inflation expectations had again moved up. *(Cleveland Fed Nowcast, Philadelphia Fed SPF Q2 2026, FOMC Minutes April 2026, updated June 10, 2026)*

**Brent crude slides to $92.34 as demand outlook darkens; oil volatility spills onto Hyperliquid**
Brent crude settled at $92.34 and WTI at $89.26 on June 11, extending a pullback from the $97–$109 range reached in May as softening demand forecasts and partial easing of Strait of Hormuz tensions weigh on the market. OPEC has already trimmed its demand-growth estimate to 1.3 million bpd for both 2025 and 2026, and the IEA continues to project a hefty oversupply of 3.7 million bpd. Notably, Hyperliquid's aggregate open interest hit $1.43 billion on June 10 with crude oil — not crypto — identified as the dominant driver, underscoring how round-the-clock decentralised perpetuals are absorbing commodity volatility. *(Reuters / NBC LA / Yahoo Finance / Hyperliquid OI data, June 10–11, 2026)*

**Gold retreats to $4,255.80 as dollar holds above 99; remains 22%+ higher year-over-year**
Spot gold fell to $4,255.80 on June 11, continuing its correction from May highs after Trading Economics reported the metal dropped 3.26% on June 10 alone and has shed roughly 13% over the past month. The DXY dollar index is holding near 99.99, consistent with its June 8 close, anchoring the pressure on gold even as the 10-year Treasury note contract ($109.219) signals that long-duration rates remain elevated. Despite the near-term weakness, gold is still approximately 22.85% higher than a year ago, and Trading Economics' macro model projects a recovery toward $4,355 by end of Q2 2026. *(Trading Economics / Yahoo Finance / MarketWatch, June 10–11, 2026)*

**Morgan Stanley flags asymmetric energy exposure: Europe most vulnerable, China least as oil shock persists**
Morgan Stanley's 2026 outlook warns that exposure to the ongoing energy shock differs sharply by region — Europe faces the fastest passthrough to household energy bills, the US sits in an intermediate position, and China is least exposed after building substantial crude stockpiles while dramatically reducing spot imports. The IMF, in its June 2026 Finance & Development piece, cautioned policymakers against enacting "crude protectionism in the guise of national security," warning that geopolitically motivated trade restrictions reduce economic efficiency even when resilience is the stated goal. SF Fed Vice Chair Òscar Jordà reiterated on June 4 that GDP growth is tracking slightly below long-run trend with risks skewed to the downside, and that market pricing implies tighter monetary policy going forward. *(Morgan Stanley 2026 Outlook / IMF F&D June 2026 / SF Fed FedViews June 4, 2026)*
